Hidden confounds in EEG emotion recognition

A controlled evaluation on the FACED dataset

The question

FACED is one of the largest public EEG emotion datasets: 123 people, 32 channels, 28 video clips and 9 emotions. The published benchmark for the hardest setting (9 classes, tested on people the model has never seen) was about 42%. My goal was to beat it.

What I built

Differential-entropy features in five frequency bands plus functional connectivity between channels, over sliding windows. On top of that: a bidirectional GRU with attention, a Transformer encoder and an order-invariant MLP, combined in a soft-voting ensemble across architectures and seeds, trained with class-weighted loss and subject-grouped cross-validation. I also ran ablations on data volume, channels and signal duration. The ensemble reached ~53%.

The turning point

A reviewer report made me ask why the number was so high. Instead of defending it, I designed control experiments and found two hidden confounds:

  1. Target-subject normalization. Scaling features with the test person's own recordings added about 17 points. The "subject-independent" result quietly needed the new person's full recording.
  2. Stimulus identity. Everyone watches the same 28 clips, and standard splits put the same clips in training and test. The EEG carries a signature of each clip, so the model recognised clips, not emotions.

Honest result

Under the strictest clean protocol (new people, new clips, train-only normalization), 9-class balanced accuracy is ~14.5%, only weakly above the 11.1% chance level (permutation p = 0.032). The remaining signal sits in a few physiologically distinct emotions, such as disgust and amusement. A manuscript is prepared.

PharmaRAG

Medicine questions answered only from official leaflets, with a source for every sentence.

1. What it is

You ask about a specific medicine in one of six languages and pick a country. PharmaRAG answers using only that country's official patient leaflet, and every sentence links to the exact passage it came from. If the leaflet doesn't cover the question, the app says so instead of making up an answer. Medical content goes through safety tiers.

2. How it works

Drug registers from six countries are grouped by active substance and form, leaflets are split by their real sections, embedded and stored in Qdrant (~400k chunks). When you ask a question, product-name detection and hybrid re-ranking find the passages, and an LLM (Groq, with Gemini as fallback) writes an answer with numbered citations. Answers come back in 2–4 seconds.

Catch it.
Sort the buckwheat.

A calm, satisfying mobile game based on a very Ukrainian chore. You drag each grain with your finger: good ones into the bowl, everything else into the bin. That means dark, shrivelled, spotted, deformed or moldy grains, and also pebbles, oat flakes, petals and specks. A right move gives +10, a mistake −5. Every level adds more grains and trickier types, and an optional timer gives a bonus for speed.

The whole game is one index.html with zero dependencies. Everything on screen is drawn with the Canvas API, and the sounds are made in code with the Web Audio API. It works with both touch and mouse, and a service worker lets it run offline and install on a phone like a native app.
